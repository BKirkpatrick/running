A strange and wonderful run.

I decided to move my long runs forward, from Sundays to Saturdays, so that my weekly schedule mimics race week (the marathon falls on a Saturday). Making that decision now meant that I avoided storm whats-its-face, but at the cost of running 25 km over 3 consecutive days.

After 2 kms I felt starving and had to dip into my pouches earlier than planned. Not ideal. I then crossed paths with a paddleboarder as I reached the first aquaduct (I never thought I would write a sentence like that). How whimsical, I thought to myself. I said "good morning", obviously.

After passing herons, ducks, goosanders, and moorhens (were they sent by Odin - because of the nice poem I wrote for that pigeon? No, he would have sent ravens. Relax) I reached a second aquaduct. I have never run far enough to reach this aquaduct before, and despite having heard legends of it I never realised it was anywhere near me.

The Scott Russell aquaduct is named for the young engineer who was researching how to make barges travel faster along the canal. In so doing he created a soliton - a wave that can travel long distances without changing its shape or speed. Russell chased his original one, on horseback, for 3 km!

I have decided to add solitons to my list of running inspirations. Whenever I need to lock-in on my form and enter cruise control, I am going to "think soliton".
